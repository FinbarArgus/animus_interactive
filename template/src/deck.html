Example

Damping sets how fast the swing dies

\[ \class{term t-acc}{\ddot x}+\class{term t-damp}{2\zeta\omega\,\dot x}+\class{term t-spring}{\omega^2x}=0 \]
t-acc
The acceleration of the mass.
t-damp
Damping: a force against the velocity, set by the damping ratio ζ.
t-spring
The spring: a force back towards rest, set by the natural frequency ω.

Natural frequency ω = rad/s, from the data.

The curve is the mass's position after it is let go from x = 1. Drag the ζ slider: small ζ rings for a long time, ζ = 1 returns fastest without overshoot, large ζ creeps back. Hover a term of the equation for its meaning.

Drag the slider or click: the first click shows critical damping (zeta = 1), the second light damping.
Part 2

A divider slide

With ζ = 1, how does the mass come back to rest?

  1. It overshoots once and then settles.That is just below ζ = 1; at ζ = 1 there is no overshoot.
  2. Fastest without any overshoot.ζ = 1 is critical damping.
  3. It rings for a long time first.That is small ζ, light damping.
  4. It never quite comes back.Every ζ > 0 returns to rest; large ζ just creeps.
